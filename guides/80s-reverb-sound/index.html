How to get the 80s reverb sound

The 80s sound is mostly a reverb sound: big, bright, shimmering spaces that make a simple synth or snare feel huge. Here is what made it, and settings that get you there today, from pop and synthwave to vaporwave and lo-fi.

What made 80s reverb sound like that

Early digital reverb units changed how records sounded. They could make rooms no real building had: very long, very smooth, often brighter than nature. Producers leaned into it, and a few habits became the sound of the decade.

  • Long, bright halls and plates. Decays of three to six seconds on pads, vocals and leads, with plenty of top end left in the tail.
  • Pre-delay. A short gap before the reverb starts keeps the dry sound clear while the space blooms behind it.
  • Movement in the tail. Digital reverbs gently modulated their reflections, which gives that shimmering, slightly chorused shine.
  • Gated reverb on drums. A huge room that cuts off suddenly after each hit: enormous, but it never washes over the next beat.
  • Chorus on everything. Guitars, keys, even vocals got a wide, detuned chorus that makes the reverb feel wider still.

Modern retro styles add one more ingredient: wear. Synthwave, vaporwave and lo-fi often sound like the music was played off an old VHS tape, with wobbling pitch, hiss and a narrowed, softened top end.

Recipes that work

Synthwave pads and leads

  • A large hall, decay 3.5 to 5 seconds.
  • Pre-delay around 30 to 60 ms, or a 1/16 note at your tempo.
  • Mix around 30 to 45%, plus a little modulation in the tail.
  • Cut the reverb's low end below about 200 Hz so the bass stays clean.

In Palm Life: Miami Nights or Neon Hall, and turn up Nostalgia for more shine.

Gated snare

  • A big, bright room on the snare only.
  • Gate the tail so it cuts off after 200 to 400 ms, before the next hit.
  • Push the reverb level higher than feels sensible. That's the sound.

In Palm Life: Big Gated Snare. The Gate reads in milliseconds and the shape display shows where it cuts.

Vocals that sit in the mix

  • A plate or medium hall, decay 2 to 3 seconds, pre-delay 60 to 90 ms.
  • Duck the reverb so it drops while the singer sings and blooms in the gaps.
  • Cut the reverb's top end a little if the "s" sounds get splashy.

In Palm Life: Lead Vocal Halo or Slow Jam Ballad, which duck already.

Vaporwave and lo-fi tape

  • Put the whole sound on "tape": slow wow, a little flutter and soft saturation.
  • Narrow the frequency range, as cheap tape does, and add hiss you can just hear.
  • Use a wide, slightly dark reverb, then slow the whole song down a few percent.

In Palm Life: Empty Mall 1989, Rewind 1986 (VHS hiss) or Taped Off The TV (TV static and buzz).

A tail that drifts

  • Auto-pan only the reverb, slowly, and keep the dry sound in the centre.
  • Lock the movement to your tempo, for example one sweep every two bars.

In Palm Life: Night Drive Pan or Headlights Sweep.

Mixing tips

  • EQ the reverb, not just the instrument. Most muddy 80s-style mixes come from low end in the reverb tail.
  • Check in mono. Very wide, chorused reverbs can thin out on phone speakers. Pull the width back a little if they do.
  • Less on the bass and kick. Keep the low end dry and let the space live in the mids and highs.
  • Automate it. More reverb in breakdowns and choruses, less in busy verses, is a very 80s move.
